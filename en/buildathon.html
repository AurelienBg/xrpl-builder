Section 00

Strategic synthesis — 3 models to arbitrate

Recommendation

Hybrid B + C · annual flagship + continuous waves

A 30-day flagship Buildathon (Colosseum model) + 2-3 continuous 14-day waves per year (Akindo model)

The 3 models are not mutually exclusive. The market shows that ecosystems producing serious founders combine a flagship event (strong signal, media coverage, deadline that forces delivery) + an ongoing program (continuous activation, permanent onboarding, milestone-based iteration).

  • Pros: year-round ecosystem activity · robust VC pipeline · deadline-driven + iteration-driven · continuous onboarding
  • Cons: 2 systems to operate (an event + a platform) · requires on-chain infrastructure for waves · more organizational overhead
The 4 market models
  • A. Sprint Hackathon
    Short format (24-72h), IRL primary. Examples: ETHGlobal Cannes/NY/Denver, Lablab.ai, ETHCC side-hackathons. Prize pool ~$300-500k fragmented in sponsor bounties ($5-20k each) across 10-20 tracks. Pros: strong visibility, easy sponsor activation, mass of participants. Cons: superficial projects, no follow-up, diluted quality, no pipeline.
  • B. Cohort Accelerator
    Medium format (30 days) + 6-8 weeks accelerator. Examples: Solana Colosseum, Anthropic Claude Code. Curated selection 30-50 teams max. Concentrated prize pool ($50-100k top 3) + pre-seed investment $250k per accelerator team (top 5-10). 1:1 mentor support, demo day, follow-on VC. Pros: funded pipeline, project quality, real follow-up (PMF, fundraise). Cons: less open (curated), more complex to operate, requires VC fund.
  • C. Continuous Buildathon (NEW)
    Long format (6 months) in continuous waves. Example: Akindo / WaveHack. Waves of 14 days (10d submit + 4d vote), 1/10 of pool distributed per wave. WaveHack Global = $500k+ to 5000+ builders over 6 months, 30+ projects continuously funded. On-chain distribution in USDC/USDT (Ethereum, Base, BNB, Astar — XRPL not yet supported). Milestone-based, not demo-based. Pros: long-term builders, continuous iteration, ongoing ecosystem activity, on-chain transparent. Cons: requires on-chain infrastructure, complex management, no marketing peak moment.
  • D. Multi-city Satellite Buildathon (NEW)
    Medium format (3 weeks) running parallel multi-city editions, online + IRL Founder House. Example: Arbitrum Open House (May 2026) — 4 editions on the same format, each city with its own pool: NYC $30k (514 participants) · Dubai $30k (73) · London $115k (230) · Bengaluru/India $70k (78, IRL hybrid) = ~$245k cumulative across 4 cities. Tech stack Solidity + Rust (Stylus). "Integrated Learning Experience" component (learning track baked into the buildathon). Top winners get a spot at an IRL Founder House (physical gathering post-build). Pros: mobilizes local communities without massive IRL cost, distributed prize pool attracts diverse geos (US/Gulf/Europe/Asia), learning track lowers entry barrier. Cons: more complex to orchestrate in parallel, dilutes the single peak moment, requires local relays per city.
How this translates

The 10 sections of v0.1 below were designed with the Model A + B lens (flagship hackathon + accelerator follow-up). Models C (Akindo waves) and D (Arbitrum multi-city) open 2 additional hybrid paths. Section 12 at the bottom of the page proposes 3 final variants for the XRPL Commons committee.

XRPL Commons journey · the Buildathon in the chain

the Buildathon fits into a broader journey within the XRPL Commons ecosystem. It is the "test on mainnet with real users" link in the chain:

📚 Academy  →  🛠 Buildathon  →  🐠 Aquarium  →  🚀 Startup Studio (coming soon)
  • The Academy prepares upstream (workshops, mentoring, educational content). The content produced during the Buildathon (recordings, office hours, learnings) should feed back into the Academy afterward.
  • The Buildathon tests builders on mainnet over 30 days, with real users and measurable on-chain activity (Section 05 gate). Target: projects reaching a few hundred to ~1000 users.
  • The Aquarium incubates top projects post-buildathon (the 1st major prize wins a direct entry — see Section 05).
  • The Startup Studio (coming soon) will support the most mature startups beyond.

Note: the idea of a Perpetual Hackathon (Akindo/WaveHack-style, cf. Model C) stays on the table as a distinct project to explore after the 2026 Buildathon — not for this edition.

Section 01

Name — what do we call this thing?

Proposal

Buildathon

Generic, sober, SEO-friendly name. The subtitle "XRPL Commons · 30 days" carries context and duration.

We keep the word everyone understands instantly, with no marketing overhead. The program leans on its mechanics (30 days, $100k hybrid pool, milestone-grants + small prizes) rather than an invented brand. The subtitle Buildathon · XRPL Commons covers the ecosystem-context need. Final communication vote stays with the marketing team.

  • Pros: instantly understood, SEO-friendly, no marketing overhead, format recognized by builders and VCs; consistent with the rest of the site (/opportunities, /interviews)
  • Cons: zero textual differentiation — every ecosystem has a Buildathon. Differentiation will come from the subtitle, the mechanics (hybrid pool, milestone-grants), and editorial execution.
Alternatives
  • Buildathon 30x
    Tagline "30 days · to the power of x" — mechanic encoded in the name (30 days × rewards to the power of x). Pros: punchy, memorable, differentiating (no other crypto brand uses this pattern: Solana=Colosseum, Arbitrum=Open House, ETHGlobal=city-based); encodes duration and signals possible amplification (30², 30³ next year). Cons: doesn't say "XRPL"; minor SEO risk; marketing overhead to explain the name mechanic.
  • ChallengeX
    Frames the event around challenges (the 67 opportunities in the /opportunities registry, 41 APP for the Buildathon) rather than the build action. The "X" nods to XRPL. Pros: challenge-centric framing aligns with the unified /opportunities registry; the X is a discreet XRPL nod. Cons: "X" is overloaded in crypto branding (xAI, X.com, etc.); risk of confusion.
Section 02

Format & cadence — how long, what rhythm?

Proposal

30 days · full online

Day 0 kickoff → 28 days build → Day 29 freeze → Day 30 Demo Day. 100% remote.

Compact 30-day sprint, fully online. No travel, no in-person logistics — every team competes on equal footing globally. Long enough to ship a credible MVP + working demo (4 weeks of build), short enough to maintain urgency.

  • Day 0: Kickoff online, presentation and Q&A session
  • Day 1 → Day 28: Build phase — 2 office-hour sessions/week (30min each)
  • Day 29: Pitch deck freeze + Demo freeze — code, deck, video demo all locked
  • Day 30: Demo Day online (livestream) — mandatory gate: project deployed on XRPL mainnet with measurable on-chain activity via SourceTag (volume, transactions, addresses). Jury review · prizes announcement.
🛠 Platform & tools — to be decided by the team

Submission · jury review · criteria tracking · prizes announcement: HACKATHON platform considered as the operational backbone.
Participant tracking · communications · community: possible complementary use of the PULSE app. Final platform decision to be arbitrated internally — depends on required features and possible integration with SourceTag for on-chain tracking.

Alternatives
  • 3 weeks online + integrated learning track (Arbitrum-style)
    Arbitrum Open House format — 21 days of build + "Integrated Learning Experience" (training modules baked into the buildathon). Pros: lowers entry barrier (beginners learn while building), 1 week shorter = sharper urgency, validated at $115k pool / 230 participants in London. Cons: 21 days may be too short to hit the "$10k on-chain volume" gate (our Section 05) — risks filtering out too many projects.
  • 14-day ultra-sprint (Akindo wave-style)
    14-day wave aligned with the Continuous model (Section 00 · Model C) — 10 days submit + 4 days vote/judging. Pros: chain multiple waves per year, continuous momentum. Cons: too short for the $10k on-chain volume gate, teams must re-form each wave.
  • 6 weeks with post-build accelerator (Colosseum-style)
    30 days build + 2 weeks demo-day prep + VC intros. Pros: time to polish, primes the VC follow-on. Cons: requires accelerator infrastructure (VC mentors, fund partnerships), loses the tight sprint urgency. Worth considering if we adopt Model B.
  • Multi-city satellite (Arbitrum Open House)
    Instead of one global buildathon, launch 3-4 parallel editions: Paris (HQ), Singapore, Dubai, NY. Each city = 3 weeks online + local IRL Founder House. See Section 00 · Model D. Pros: mobilizes local XRPL communities, distributed prize pools reach more geos. Cons: complex parallel orchestration, requires local relays.

Section 04

Challenge register — what can teams build?

Proposal

Registry /opportunities — 41 APP opportunities, 5 themes

The Buildathon challenge register = the APP subset of the unified registry /opportunities (single source of truth · convergence PWG + ATKA + ECO + YC + interviews). The 26 TOOL opps (libraries, primitives, SDKs) are out of scope for the Buildathon — intended for existing teams / core contributors.
🟢 Key message to builders

The OPPs in the registry are identified opportunities — ideas where we believe a potential need exists (converging signals from PWG + ATKA + ecosystem + YC + founder interviews). They are in no way a restrictive framework. All XRPL projects are allowed and welcome — whether they map to an OPP, combine several, or propose something completely different. The registry is a starting point for builders looking for inspiration, not a mandatory checklist.

→ If your project comes from a previously started project, that's totally OK. No obligation to start from scratch on Day 0 — an existing project that ships a new mainnet milestone during the 30-day Buildathon is just as eligible. The only criterion that matters is the Section 05 gate (mainnet deployment + measurable on-chain activity by day 30).

→ Realistic ambition: we target projects reaching a few hundred real users on mainnet by end of the 30 days, up to ~1000 users for the most ambitious. no need to aim for 10k+ users to win.

Teams choose a challenge from the 41 APP opportunities in the registry, spread across 5 themes (builder-primitives is now 100% TOOL → out of scope for the buildathon): Wallets (6) · B2B (6) · RWAT (7) · DeFi (13) · AI Agents (9). Prize bracket mapped on the 3 registry tiers:

  • Tier HIGH (×7 APP challenges) → Grand Prix Bracket. The highest-impact user-facing opps:
    • OPP-017 · B2B Stablecoin FX desk / corridor (b2b)
    • OPP-030 · Lending Protocol on EVM Sidechain (Morpho-style) (defi)
    • OPP-034 · Lending front-end on Single Asset Vault (pre-XLS-66) (defi)
    • OPP-025 · Compliance-native consumer payments (Wise-on-XRPL) (rwat)
    • OPP-036 · Restaking Protocol for XRP (Babylon-fork) (defi)
    • OPP-019 · XRPL-native dApp analytics platform (DappRadar + Dune) (b2b)
    • OPP-043 · Liquid Staked XRP (LST · Lido-equivalent) (defi)
  • Tier MEDIUM (×23 APP challenges) → Major Prize. User-facing opps with strong demand (community survey · regulatory window · top-of-mind founders).
  • Tier LOW (×11 APP challenges) → Standard Prize. Lower-priority user-facing opps (specific verticals, novelties).
  • Open category: teams can propose an off-registry APP challenge if justified; jury decides eligibility. If retained and impactful, the opportunity is added to the registry.

→ The registry is alive: founder interviews (Djason ✓ · Xavier ✓ · Florian ✓ · Florence ✓ · Guillaume ✓ · Stan/Kevin/Adrien/Denis/Thomas/Romain/Andy/Odelia upcoming) add opps with tag INT-{name}. Snapshot above corresponds to May 2026 — full registry 67 opps (41 APP + 26 TOOL).

Alternatives
  • Curated short-list (top 10 challenges)
    Keep only the 7 multipliers + 3 wildcards = 10 max challenges. Pros: easier to communicate, simpler decision for builders, extreme focus. Cons: excludes 46+ valid opportunities; risk that builders pivot to a sub-optimal or off-registry challenge.
  • Open-form (no register)
    Any XRPL-related project welcome, no constraint. Pros: max creativity. Cons: wastes all the registry curation work (PWG + ATKA + ECO + YC + interviews); jury has nothing to anchor on; risk of generic projects ("yet another DEX").
  • Sponsor-driven challenges
    Each major sponsor (Ripple, Mastercard, Securitize, Anodos) defines their own challenge — ETHGlobal model. Pros: guarantees sponsor engagement, fragmented prize pool easy to fundraise. Cons: challenges may not align with /opportunities; "sponsored content" risk; complicates communication to builders.
  • Theme filter (5 parallel tracks)
    A separate competition per theme (Wallets, B2B, RWAT, DeFi, AI Agents — the 5 APP themes, Builder Primitives out of scope as 100% TOOL). Each theme has its own Grand Prix + thematic jury. Pros: recruits specialist judges per theme; clean read for "DeFi only" or "wallets only" builders. Cons: dilutes cross-thematic multipliers (Pre-Deployed Testnet helps all themes).
Section 05

Prizes — what do winners get?

Proposal

Pool $100k · Hybrid milestone-grants + small prizes + Aquarium

2026 annual dedicated budget — $100k structured in 3 brackets: $50k in major milestone-grants ($10k × 5) + $50k in small prizes (€500 × up to 100 projects) + Aquarium access for 1st place.
Eligibility gate — mandatory for any prize
  • Project deployed on XRPL mainnet by day 30
  • Measurable on-chain activity by day 30 via SourceTag: volume · transactions · unique addresses

Filters out polished demos, rewards real traction (mainnet + users + volume). Inspired by the "RFS + live traction" model (Florence interview).

🥇 Major prizes · Milestone Grants $10k × 5

5 major prizes of $10k each, structured as milestone-based grants to ensure long-term engagement. Progressive payout:

Milestone When Amount
Upfront Results announcement (Day 30) $3,000
Milestone M1 ~3 months post-buildathon (Oct 2026) $3,000
Milestone M2 ~6 months post-buildathon (Jan 2027) $4,000
Total / project $10,000

Milestones M1/M2 are defined on technical + traction criteria (co-built with each winning team after Day 30).

🏆 Aquarium Access · for the 1st major prize

Top 1 wins, in addition to the $10k milestone-grant, a direct entry to the Aquarium — XRPL Commons incubation program. Stepping stone to Startup Studio (coming soon).

🎟 Small prizes · €500 × up to 100 projects

No hard cap on the number. Every project that passes the live-traction gate receives a small prize of €500. Dedicated pool of $50k — up to 100 qualifying projects. If more projects qualify, jury arbitrates on secondary engagement (on-chain volume, user count, retention).

Total budget = exactly $100k 5 × $10k major milestone-grants ($50k) + up to 100 × €500 small prizes ($50k) + Aquarium access (perk, no extra cash). 2026 annual pool — possible multi-edition if renewed next year.
Alternatives
  • Pool $30k · Volume Prize (former proposal)
    $30k pool distributed in equal shares to any project exceeding $10k cumulative volume. Pros: ultra-simple, sharp volume gate. Cons: misses early-stage projects without volume yet, excludes serious founders who take longer. Committee preferred the hybrid milestone-grants + small prizes to cover both profiles.
  • 3 fixed tiers — €47k pool (former proposal)
    Tier 1 €5k×3 + Tier 2 €3k×6 + Tier 3 €1k×8. Pros: clear bracket, predictable. Cons: rewards the demo, not traction. Florence: "broad buildathons only work on dense ecosystems — XRPL isn't there yet".
  • Users prize — onboarding-based (former proposal)
    $1,000 for 1k users onboarded + $10,000 for 10k users. Pros: easy to measure, growth signal. Cons: bots easy to fabricate, onboarding ≠ retention. Cumulative USD volume is more rigorous and harder to game.
  • Smaller cash pool (€60k)
    Larger pool: Tier 1 €15k×3, Tier 2 €5k×3, Tier 3 €2k×8. Pros: attracts more serious builders, more competitive vs other hackathons. Cons: requires more sponsorship budget.
  • Equity/token pool
    Winners get Ripple investment commitment instead of cash. Pros: aligns long-term incentives, more capital. Cons: complex legals, dilutes ownership, adds friction.
  • Equal split (no tiers)
    Top 10 teams each get €15k flat. Pros: egalitarian, removes "winner's curse". Cons: removes incentive to push for excellence; Tier 1 multipliers should be rewarded more.
  • Cash + 5% revenue royalty (3y)
    Winners get cash + a 5% royalty on revenue for 3 years (capped). Pros: long-term aligned, recoverable for ecosystem. Cons: complex, deterring for some builders. Probably overkill for a buildathon.
  • IRL Founder House post-buildathon (Arbitrum-style)
    Top 5-10 winners get a spot at a physical Founder House (3-5 days in Paris at the Aquarium, or Singapore/NY if multi-city) with mentors, VC intros, private demo days. Inspired by Arbitrum Open House. Pros: cash + tangible perks + dense networking, turns a buildathon into an accelerator pipeline. Cons: logistical cost (flights/lodging), requires dedicated physical venue. Stackable with the Volume Prize above.

Section 07

Judging criteria — how is "winning" measured?

Proposal

2-step judging — Binary gate, then bracket-specific scoring

Criteria apply differently depending on the prize bracket (small prizes vs major milestone-grants). All published to candidates upfront.

⛔ Step 1 — Live-traction gate (binary · mandatory for any prize)

All-or-nothing criteria by Day 30. Without these 2 conditions, no prize is awarded (cf. Section 05):

  • Mainnet deployment — project deployed and accessible on XRPL mainnet, source code published (or justified if proprietary)
  • Measurable on-chain activity via SourceTag — at least 1 real transaction attributable to the project via SourceTag, verifiable by the jury on XRPL explorer

🎟 Step 2a — Small prizes €500 (simple gate-based scoring)

Any project that passes the gate automatically receives the €500 small prize — no secondary scoring (unless the pool saturates). If more than 100 projects qualify, jury arbitrates on engagement metrics (descending order):

  1. Cumulative on-chain volume (USD-equivalent) over the 30 days
  2. Unique addresses having interacted with the project
  3. Transaction count attributable

🥇 Step 2b — Major prizes $10k milestone-grants (multi-criteria scoring)

5 major prizes awarded based on weighted scoring among projects that passed the gate. Total = 100%.

Criterion What it measures Weight
🚀 On-chain traction Cumulative volume (USD-eq) · unique addresses · transaction count · retention (return-users) — objective metrics via SourceTag 40%
💎 Product quality Smooth UX/UI, feature completeness, docs, accessibility (mobile/desktop), overall polish 20%
⚙️ Tech execution Code quality, security, architecture, audit-readiness (smart contracts if applicable), open-source preferred 15%
🌱 Long-term commit Clear post-Day 30 roadmap, capacity to execute milestones M1/M2 over 6 months, legal structure underway or ready 15%
📣 Pitch & narrative Video demo clarity (60-120s), one-pager deck, why-now, story-telling 10%
Total 100%

🏆 Aquarium Access · top 1 only

The project ranked #1 (highest weighted score across the 5 criteria) wins, in addition to the milestone-grant, the direct Aquarium entry (cf. Section 05).

🔄 Milestones M1/M2 post-Day 30 (unlock criteria for $3k + $4k payments)

The 5 major prizes get $3k upfront at Day 30, then $3k at M1 and $4k at M2 under conditions. Criteria co-built with each team in the 2 weeks post-Day 30 — aligned with their own roadmap. Standard frames:

  • M1 (3 months) — typically: traction doubled (users / volume), N+1 feature roadmap shipped, first partnership / integration signed, legal structure finalized
  • M2 (6 months) — typically: clear business threshold reached (e.g., annualized revenue, # tx/month, 30-day retention), first PMF signal (4-week cohort retention > X%), Aquarium application/integration if not already secured

Milestones are objective measurable, not vague. XRPL Commons validates completion before payment unlock.

Section 09 · NEW

Office Hours — team support during the build

Proposal

Weekly "ship + visibility" cadence + 2 fixed slots + 1 IRL day at the Commons House

During the 4-week build phase (Day 1 → Day 28, 17 Jun → 14 Jul)

🚢 Weekly Ship Cadence (Guillaume)

  • Each team ships every week: (1) short video (60-120s) of progress · (2) single-slide deck · (3) user feedback on socials (X post with connected wallet + XRP profile for traceability)
  • XRPL Commons amplifies: weekly push of the top 5 or 10 on its channels + single public link to view all submissions
  • Merch reward for the best comments / feedback received by each team (qualified-feedback verification: connected wallet, active XRP profile, no bots)

🎤 Fixed Office Hours + IRL

  • 🏠 Build at the Commons House — 1 day per week, come build your project at our office. IRL coworking open to Paris-based teams (or in transit), in-person access to XRPL Commons mentors, lunch on site. Fixed day TBD (likely Tuesday or Thursday, aligned with one of the office hours).
  • Tuesday 11:00–11:30 CET — Tech Office Hour (XRPL devrel + Ripple infra). Topics: XLS-66 / XLS-65 / XLS-70 specs, code review, integration debugging. Live group call (Zoom), recorded.
  • Thursday 16:00–16:30 CET — Product / GTM Office Hour (XRPL Commons + Aquarium alum). Topics: positioning, pitch shaping, distribution strategy, RLUSD/CEX integrations. Live group call, recorded.
  • On-demand AMA — async via dedicated Discord channel. ≤24h response from a rotating mentor pool of ~10 people.

👨‍🏫 1:1 Mentorship + community founder intros (Guillaume)

  • Dedicated Mentor sessions (in addition to collective office hours) — each team matched with a referent mentor for 30min/week. Bookable via shared Calendly. Covers product strategy, IP/legal, fundraising prep.
  • Founder intros from the XRPL Commons community — each team can request 2-3 qualified intros (Aquarium alumni, EU/SEA ecosystem, RLUSD partners). Goal: direct dealflow, not surface networking.
  • Recorded archive of all collective sessions, accessible to all teams + future reference.

Why this cadence: creates a public weekly heartbeat (teams ship and the market sees it), paired with deep support (1:1 mentor + IRL Commons House). The merch + XRPL Commons amplification gamify user engagement WITHOUT relying solely on on-chain volume (Section 05 gate) — filtering out bots without killing early-stage projects that don't yet have volume.

Alternatives
  • Daily 30-min standup
    Every weekday morning, all teams join. Pros: high engagement, daily momentum. Cons: heavy on mentors (5h/week), risks performative attendance, hard for global timezones.
  • Async Discord-only
    No live calls, dedicated channel + mentors check in. Pros: lightweight, timezone-friendly. Cons: no "magic moment" of live problem solving, lower energy, cohort doesn't bond.
  • Weekly group AMA
    One 90-min session per week with all teams + 4 mentors panel. Pros: minimal overhead. Cons: doesn't scale (too many teams = no time per team), 1-week feedback loop too slow for a 4-week build.
  • Office Hours by theme
    One slot per Master Atlas theme per week (Wallets, Lending, RWA, EVM, etc.) with theme-expert mentors. Pros: deep theme expertise. Cons: 6 weekly slots = mentor scheduling nightmare; teams in different themes don't cross-pollinate.
  • On-demand only (no fixed slots)
    Teams book mentors via Calendly when needed. Pros: max flexibility. Cons: no cohort heartbeat, mentor utilization unpredictable, shy teams under-use it.

Section 12

🎯 Final variants — committee choice

Recommendation

Variant 1 · Buildathon · Hybrid milestone-grants + XRPL Commons journey

Simple format, $100k annual pool, milestone-based, integrated in the Academy → Aquarium → Startup Studio journey

Format:

  • 30-day online Buildathon · open (no curated selection) · target a few hundred → ~1000 users on mainnet per project · mandatory live-traction gate (mainnet deployed + measurable on-chain activity via SourceTag by day 30)
  • $100k pool · hybrid structure (cf. Section 05):
    • 5 major prizes × $10k milestone-based ($3k upfront + $3k M1 + $4k M2) = $50k
    • Up to 100 small prizes × €500 = $50k
    • Aquarium access (perk) for the 1st major prize
  • Weekly "ship + visibility" cadence · weekly delivery (video + deck + X feedback) · Commons amplification top 5-10 · merch reward for best feedback · 1 IRL day/week at the Commons House (cf. Section 09)
  • XRPL Commons journey integration · content produced (workshops, mentoring, office hours) feeds the Academy · top 1 major prize enters the Aquarium directly · continuation to Startup Studio (coming soon)

Pros: simple to operate (1 FTE is enough) · pool focused on real traction (not demo) · milestone-grants engage winners long-term · Aquarium access = real reward beyond cash · format renewable (multi-edition possible if successful).

Cons: fewer flagship moments than Colosseum-style · no big "$300k pool" headline · depends on candidate flow (open vs curated). Mitigated by: Buildathon marketing, Commons weekly amplification, upstream Academy that prepares the funnel.

Estimated cost: ~$120-150k for the 1st edition (pool $100k + ops/tooling/marketing). Possible multi-edition next year if renewed. Very low overhead vs Variants 2/3.

Alternatives
  • Variant 2 · Buildathon Standard (Colosseum-pure)
    Annual 30-day Buildathon only, no continuous waves. Curated selection 30-50 teams, prize pool $300-500k, 8-week accelerator for top 5 with $250k pre-seed. Pros: simple to understand · aligned with proven Colosseum model · modest staffing (1-2 FTE) · cost ~$500-700k/year. Cons: annual one-shot · no activity between editions · peak-and-trough marketing · onboarding limited to the flagship 30 days.
  • Variant 3 · Builders Cohort (no public hackathon)
    No public hackathon. Direct selection of 10-15 founders/month on application (DD + interview). 8 weeks intensive cohort · $100-250k investment per team · target PMF (not demo). Pros: maximum quality · very strong VC pipeline · high signal-to-noise · total alignment with /opportunities. Cons: less ecosystem visibility · no "strong moment" to communicate · higher entry barrier · resembles a founder program more than an open event.
To decide

The choice depends on 3 political questions:

  1. Ambition: does XRPL Commons want a notable annual event (V2) or year-round ongoing activity (V1) or a founders program without public visibility (V3)?
  2. Annual budget: ~$500k (V2) · ~$700k-1.2M (V1) · ~$300-500k (V3) · plus staffing.
  3. Operational capacity: 1-2 FTE (V2 or V3) · 2-3 FTE (V1).

→ If XRPL Commons is ready to invest long-term + multi-channel: V1. If limited resources + need to prove the formula: V2. If the goal is 10 XRPL startups funded in 18 months and not ecosystem marketing: V3.
