Section 01

Name — what do we call this thing?

Recommended

XRPL Zone Build

Tagline: "Where institutional DeFi gets built"

Capitalizes on the "XRPL Zone" brand asset already established by the 4 Paris Product Working Groups (April 2026). Avoids fragmenting the comms strategy. The "Build" suffix signals action and continuity.

  • Pros: brand recognition, narrative continuity, low marketing cost
  • Cons: sounds like a continuation not an event; doesn't signal the time-bounded nature of a buildathon
Alternatives
  • 30x30
    Reference to SXSW (South by Southwest) format + perfect fit with the 30 opportunities × 30 days reading of the Master Atlas. Punchy, memorable, viral-friendly. Pros: punchy, brandable, marketing gold. Cons: doesn't say "XRPL" anywhere — needs a sub-tag like "30x30 — XRPL Buildathon".
  • Build the Institutional Chain
    Direct quote of the strategic thesis (Atka + Analysis 3). Pros: very clear positioning, attracts the right audience (institutional founders). Cons: long, declarative, less "warm" than the others.
  • XRPL Compliance Stack
    Position purely on the differentiator (compliance-native DeFi). Pros: very differentiated, niche-specific. Cons: too narrow; excludes RWA, Lending, Wallets which are part of the Atlas.
  • XRPL Buildathon Paris 2026
    The most generic, descriptive option. Pros: instantly understood, SEO-friendly. Cons: zero brand differentiation, sounds like every other crypto event.
Section 02

Format & cadence — how long, what rhythm?

Recommended

Hybrid 6-week sprint

Kickoff Paris (2 days, in person) → 4 weeks remote build → Demo Day Paris (1 day)

Sweet spot between virality (short enough to maintain momentum) and depth (long enough to ship Tier 1 multipliers, which need 8–14 weeks for full implementations — but 4 weeks is enough for a credible MVP + working demo).

  • Week 0 (pre-flight): applications open, team formation, challenge selection
  • Week 1: Kickoff Paris — 2 days in person, spec writing, mentor matching
  • Weeks 2–4: Remote build, 2 office-hour sessions/week, mentoring
  • Week 5: Code freeze, polish, docs, demo prep
  • Week 6: Demo Day Paris (live + livestream), jury, prizes
Alternatives
  • 72-hour hackathon
    Friday → Sunday, all in person, classic format. Pros: viral, FOMO-inducing, spectacular demos. Cons: only Tier 3 ships are realistic; Tier 1 multipliers need more time. Misses the "real product" goal.
  • 9-week residency (Aquarium-style)
    Full residency, like the existing XRPL Commons Aquarium DeFi cohort. Pros: deep work, real Tier 1 ships possible. Cons: overlaps heavily with Aquarium program; cannibalizes resources; longer time = lower urgency.
  • 4-month distributed
    Teams pick their challenge, build at their own pace, monthly check-ins. Pros: max flexibility, suits remote teams. Cons: low energy, hard to coordinate Demo Day, attention bleeds.
  • Staggered themes (6 weekends)
    One weekend per theme of the Master Atlas (Wallets, Institutional Infra, Lending, RWA, EVM Sidechain, Dev Tooling). Pros: covers the full Atlas, builders pick their theme. Cons: fragmented audience, harder to brand as one event.

Section 04

Challenge register — what can teams build?

Recommended

Master Atlas — 30 opportunities, 7 themes

Direct mapping from Analysis 3 → Buildathon Tier 1 / Tier 2 / Tier 3

The Master Atlas already has the convergence of 4 PWGs + Atka + external challenger built in. Mapping is straightforward:

  • Tier 1 — Multipliers (×8 challenges): M-01, M-05, M-09, M-11, M-14, M-17, M-21, M-22 → Grand Prize bracket
  • Tier 2 — High-priority (×14 challenges): M-02, M-03, M-06, M-07, M-12, M-12b, M-13, M-16b, M-18, M-19, M-20, M-24, M-25, M-26 → Major Prize
  • Tier 3 — Medium-priority (×8 challenges): M-04, M-08, M-10, M-15, M-16, M-23, M-27, M-28, M-29, M-30 → Standard Prize (some are A1 contributions)
  • Open category: teams can propose an off-Atlas challenge if they justify it; jury decides eligibility
Alternatives
  • Curated short-list (10 challenges)
    Pick 10 highest-priority items only (the 8 multipliers + 2 wildcards). Pros: easier to communicate, simpler decision for builders. Cons: excludes 20+ valid opportunities; risks builders pivoting to a sub-optimal challenge.
  • Open-form (no register)
    Any XRPL-related project welcome, no constraints. Pros: max creativity. Cons: wastes the entire Master Atlas curation work; jury has nothing to anchor against.
  • Sponsor-driven challenges
    Each major sponsor (Ripple, Mastercard, Securitize, Anodos) defines their own challenge. Pros: guarantees sponsor engagement. Cons: challenges may not align with the Master Atlas; risk of "sponsored content" feel.

Alternatives
  • Smaller cash pool (€60k)
    Larger pool: Tier 1 €15k×3, Tier 2 €5k×3, Tier 3 €2k×8. Pros: attracts more serious builders, more competitive vs other hackathons. Cons: requires more sponsorship budget.
  • Equity/token pool
    Winners get Ripple investment commitment instead of cash. Pros: aligns long-term incentives, more capital. Cons: complex legals, dilutes ownership, adds friction.
  • Equal split (no tiers)
    Top 10 teams each get €15k flat. Pros: egalitarian, removes "winner's curse". Cons: removes incentive to push for excellence; Tier 1 multipliers should be rewarded more.
  • Cash + 5% revenue royalty (3y)
    Winners get cash + a 5% royalty on revenue for 3 years (capped). Pros: long-term aligned, recoverable for ecosystem. Cons: complex, deterring for some builders. Probably overkill for a buildathon.
Section 06

Jury composition — who decides?

Recommended

4 jury members — ecosystem balanced

Representing 4 perspectives: ecosystem, infra, alumni, external
  • 1 · XRPL Commons — ecosystem perspective, knows builders + Atlas roadmap
  • 2 · Ripple devrel or RLUSD lead — institutional infra perspective, validates technical-fit
  • 3 · Successful Aquarium alum (founder, repeat builder) — practitioner perspective
  • 4 · External institutional — asset manager, CEX exec, or bank crypto desk lead

This composition balances internal advocacy (XRPL Commons, Ripple) with external accountability (alum + institutional). No single stakeholder can capture the jury. Note: with 4 members, ties are possible — the chair (XRPL Commons) holds the deciding vote.

Alternatives
  • 3 jury members (lean)
    XRPL Commons + Ripple + 1 external. Pros: faster decisions, easier scheduling. Cons: too internal, lacks credibility for an external audience.
  • 7+ jury members (broad)
    Adds Mastercard, Securitize, dev tool partner, etc. Pros: max ecosystem coverage. Cons: hard to schedule, dilutes accountability, "design by committee" risk.
  • Public vote (community-judged)
    Open vote on Twitter/Discord post-Demo Day. Pros: viral, engages community. Cons: popularity contest, gameable, inappropriate for technical evaluation.
  • Hybrid: 4 jury members + 1 community vote
    4-member panel for Tier 1 + Tier 2, community vote for Tier 3 / "People's Choice". Pros: best of both, engages community without compromising rigor. Cons: adds complexity to the announcement.

Publishing the criteria upfront with weights reduces "Devpost roulette" feel and lets candidates self-prioritize their build.

Alternatives
  • 3 criteria only (simple)
    Tech (40%), Product (30%), Pitch (30%). Pros: simple to communicate. Cons: loses the gap-fit signal; risks builders shipping cool stuff that doesn't address ecosystem priorities.
  • No weights (qualitative)
    List criteria but no numeric weights — jury members use judgment. Pros: flexibility. Cons: opaque, candidates can't optimize, looks unprofessional.
  • Tier-specific criteria
    Tier 1 emphasizes multiplier impact; Tier 3 emphasizes execution polish. Pros: tailored. Cons: complex, may confuse cross-tier comparison.
Section 08

Team composition — size & profile?

Recommended

2 to 5 people — at least 1 senior dev + 1 BD/founder

Solo allowed but no Tier 1 prize for solo entries
  • Minimum 2 people for Tier 1 + Tier 2 prize eligibility (forces dev/BD pairing)
  • Maximum 5 people to avoid "team of 10 hides individual contribution" problem
  • At least 1 senior developer (verified via CV / GitHub) — gates the technical execution criterion
  • Geographic diversity encouraged — Paris in-person for kickoff + Demo Day, remote for the build phase
  • Aquarium / Hackathon alumni get +5% application priority (existing community signal)
Alternatives
  • Solo allowed for all tiers
    No team requirement. Pros: max accessibility. Cons: dev-only or BD-only solo entries usually fail at Demo Day; better to force the pairing.
  • Team size 2–10
    Higher max, more flexibility. Pros: larger ambitious projects possible. Cons: coordination overhead, individual contribution unclear, prize money diluted.
  • Pre-formed teams only (no matching)
    Apply as full team or not at all. Pros: faster ramp, known dynamics. Cons: excludes solo applicants who haven't found cofounders yet.
  • Match-making service
    XRPL Commons runs a Discord channel + LinkedIn flow to help solos form teams. Pros: inclusive, generates ecosystem connections. Cons: ops cost, risks last-minute team chaos.
Section 09 · NEW

Office Hours — team support during the build

Recommended

2 fixed slots/week + on-demand AMA

During the 4-week build phase (Jun 17 → Jul 12)
  • Tuesday 11:00–12:00 CET — Tech Office Hour (XRPL devrel + Ripple infra). Topics: XLS-66 / XLS-65 / XLS-70 specs, code review, integration debugging. Live group call (Zoom), recorded.
  • Thursday 16:00–17:00 CET — Product / GTM Office Hour (XRPL Commons + Aquarium alum). Topics: positioning, pitch shaping, distribution strategy, RLUSD/CEX integrations. Live group call, recorded.
  • On-demand AMA — async via dedicated Discord channel. ≤24h response from a rotating mentor pool of ~10 people.
  • 1:1 office hours bookable via shared Calendly (max 30min/week per team) — for product strategy, IP/legal, fundraising prep.
  • Recorded archive of all sessions, accessible to all teams + future reference.

Why 2 fixed slots: creates a heartbeat for the cohort, makes mentor scheduling sane, lets teams plan their week. The async Discord covers everything else.

Alternatives
  • Daily 30-min standup
    Every weekday morning, all teams join. Pros: high engagement, daily momentum. Cons: heavy on mentors (5h/week), risks performative attendance, hard for global timezones.
  • Async Discord-only
    No live calls, dedicated channel + mentors check in. Pros: lightweight, timezone-friendly. Cons: no "magic moment" of live problem solving, lower energy, cohort doesn't bond.
  • Weekly group AMA
    One 90-min session per week with all teams + 4 mentors panel. Pros: minimal overhead. Cons: doesn't scale (too many teams = no time per team), 1-week feedback loop too slow for a 4-week build.
  • Office Hours by theme
    One slot per Master Atlas theme per week (Wallets, Lending, RWA, EVM, etc.) with theme-expert mentors. Pros: deep theme expertise. Cons: 6 weekly slots = mentor scheduling nightmare; teams in different themes don't cross-pollinate.
  • On-demand only (no fixed slots)
    Teams book mentors via Calendly when needed. Pros: max flexibility. Cons: no cohort heartbeat, mentor utilization unpredictable, shy teams under-use it.

Open questions for the committee

Decisions that don't fit into the 9 main sections
  • Visual identity — does the Buildathon get its own logo (variant of XB with "Build" suffix)? Or stays under the XRPL Builder umbrella?
  • Application platform — Devpost, Gitcoin, Typeform, or custom XRPL form? Determines the pre-flight cost + integration with Discord.
  • Mentor pool size & recruitment — target ~15 mentors (10 active + 5 backup). Mix of XRPL Commons / Ripple / Aquarium alums / external partners. Who recruits, how compensated?
  • Sponsorship outreach — Ripple (likely yes), Mastercard (RLUSD partnership existing), Securitize / Anodos (RWA), Evernorth (XLS-66). Who owns the convo, what tier of sponsorship?
  • Communication strategy — who announces, when (15 July hard deadline), through which channels (XRPL Twitter, Ripple Insights, devrel newsletters, crypto press)? Is there a "wave 1" in July + "wave 2" in September?
  • Demo Day format — pitch length (5min? 8min?), fishbowl Q&A or judges-only Q&A, livestream with audience interaction?
  • IP & licensing — does the Buildathon require open-source MIT/Apache for prize eligibility, or is closed-source allowed (with the team retaining IP)?
