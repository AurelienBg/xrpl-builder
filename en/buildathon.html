Section 01

Name — what do we call this thing?

Proposal

Building zone

Tagline: "Where institutional DeFi gets built"

Short, action-oriented, evokes a workspace where building happens. Continuity with the "XRPL Zone" Paris PWG brand asset (April 2026) without being too tied to it — works as a standalone event name.

  • Pros: punchy, action verb, neutral enough to scale beyond a single event, low marketing cost
  • Cons: doesn't say "XRPL" — needs a sub-tag for context (e.g. "Building zone · XRPL")
Alternatives
  • 30x30
    Reference to SXSW (South by Southwest) format + perfect fit with the 30 opportunities × 30 days reading of the Master Atlas. Punchy, memorable, viral-friendly. Pros: punchy, brandable, marketing gold. Cons: doesn't say "XRPL" anywhere — needs a sub-tag like "30x30 — XRPL Buildathon".
  • Buildathon
    The generic format name, no embellishment. Pros: instantly understood, SEO-friendly, no marketing overhead. Cons: zero differentiation — every ecosystem has a Buildathon. Risk of being indistinguishable from competing events.
  • ChallengeX
    Frames the event around challenges (the 42 opportunities in the Master Gaps register) rather than the build action. The "X" nods to XRPL. Pros: challenge-centric framing aligns with the curated register; the X is a discreet XRPL nod. Cons: "X" is overloaded in crypto branding (xAI, X.com, etc.); risk of confusion.
Section 02

Format & cadence — how long, what rhythm?

Proposal

30 days · full online

Day 0 kickoff → 28 days build → Day 29 freeze → Day 30 Demo Day. 100% remote.

Compact 30-day sprint, fully online. No travel, no in-person logistics — every team competes on equal footing globally. Long enough to ship a credible MVP + working demo (4 weeks of build), short enough to maintain urgency.

  • Day 0: Kickoff online — spec writing, mentor matching, challenge confirmation
  • Day 1 → Day 28: Build phase — 2 office-hour sessions/week (30min each), async mentoring on Discord
  • Day 29: Pitch deck freeze + Demo freeze — code, deck, video demo all locked
  • Day 30: Demo Day online (livestream), jury review, prizes announcement

Section 04

Challenge register — what can teams build?

Proposal

Master Atlas — 30 opportunities, 7 themes

Direct mapping from Analysis 3 → Buildathon Tier 1 / Tier 2 / Tier 3

The Master Atlas already has the convergence of 4 PWGs + Atka + external challenger built in. Mapping is straightforward:

  • Tier 1 — Multipliers (×8 challenges): M-01, M-05, M-09, M-11, M-14, M-17, M-21, M-22 → Grand Prize bracket
  • Tier 2 — High-priority (×14 challenges): M-02, M-03, M-06, M-07, M-12, M-12b, M-13, M-16b, M-18, M-19, M-20, M-24, M-25, M-26 → Major Prize
  • Tier 3 — Medium-priority (×8 challenges): M-04, M-08, M-10, M-15, M-16, M-23, M-27, M-28, M-29, M-30 → Standard Prize (some are A1 contributions)
  • Open category: teams can propose an off-Atlas challenge if they justify it; jury decides eligibility
Alternatives
  • Curated short-list (10 challenges)
    Pick 10 highest-priority items only (the 8 multipliers + 2 wildcards). Pros: easier to communicate, simpler decision for builders. Cons: excludes 20+ valid opportunities; risks builders pivoting to a sub-optimal challenge.
  • Open-form (no register)
    Any XRPL-related project welcome, no constraints. Pros: max creativity. Cons: wastes the entire Master Atlas curation work; jury has nothing to anchor against.
  • Sponsor-driven challenges
    Each major sponsor (Ripple, Mastercard, Securitize, Anodos) defines their own challenge. Pros: guarantees sponsor engagement. Cons: challenges may not align with the Master Atlas; risk of "sponsored content" feel.

Users prize (any team that demonstrates real onboarding):

  • Users Prize — $1,000 for any project that onboards 1,000 users during or by end of build phase
  • Super Users Prize — $10,000 for any project that onboards 10,000 users

From the community prize — €500 for winners who already came through one of our previous events / programs (XRPL Commons Training, Aquarium Residency, prior Hackathons). Recognizes ecosystem loyalty.

Alternatives
  • Smaller cash pool (€60k)
    Larger pool: Tier 1 €15k×3, Tier 2 €5k×3, Tier 3 €2k×8. Pros: attracts more serious builders, more competitive vs other hackathons. Cons: requires more sponsorship budget.
  • Equity/token pool
    Winners get Ripple investment commitment instead of cash. Pros: aligns long-term incentives, more capital. Cons: complex legals, dilutes ownership, adds friction.
  • Equal split (no tiers)
    Top 10 teams each get €15k flat. Pros: egalitarian, removes "winner's curse". Cons: removes incentive to push for excellence; Tier 1 multipliers should be rewarded more.
  • Cash + 5% revenue royalty (3y)
    Winners get cash + a 5% royalty on revenue for 3 years (capped). Pros: long-term aligned, recoverable for ecosystem. Cons: complex, deterring for some builders. Probably overkill for a buildathon.
Section 06

Jury composition — who decides?

Proposal

XRPL Commons (only)

Single-organization jury — clean ownership, fast decisions

XRPL Commons runs the jury alone. Knows the builders, knows the Atlas roadmap, owns the program quality. No coordination overhead, no scheduling nightmare, clear accountability if a result is contested.

  • Pros: simple, fast, clear ownership, no scheduling friction
  • Cons: no external advocacy/accountability balance — risk of being seen as too internal
Alternatives
  • + Ripple (devrel or RLUSD lead)
    XRPL Commons + Ripple. Pros: adds institutional infra perspective, RLUSD/XLS-66 expertise. Cons: coordination cost ; perception of Ripple bias.
  • + Sponsor partners (those funding prizes)
    Each sponsor that funds a prize gets a jury seat. Currently potential sponsors: Mastercard (RLUSD merchant prize), Securitize/Anodos (RWA prize), Evernorth (XLS-66 prize). Pros: aligns governance with funding. Cons: sponsor biases on their own challenge categories.
Section 07

Judging criteria — how is "winning" measured?

Proposal

4 criteria — simple & weighted

Total = 100%, published to candidates upfront
CriterionWhat it measuresWeight
TechCode quality, security, architecture, working build30%
ProductUX, completeness, docs, demo polish25%
TractionReal onboarding, signups, usage demonstrated during the 30 days25%
PitchDemo video clarity, narrative, why-now20%

Simple to communicate, easy to optimize as a team, no overlap. Traction is the new lever (replacing "Distribution thinking" + "Gap fit" combined): in a 30-day full-online format, real signups and demonstrated usage are the most reliable signal of fit.

Section 09 · NEW

Office Hours — team support during the build

Proposal

2 fixed slots/week · 30 min each

During the 4-week build phase (Day 1 → Day 28, 25 Jun → 22 Jul)
  • Tuesday 11:00–11:30 CET — Tech Office Hour (XRPL devrel + Ripple infra). Topics: XLS-66 / XLS-65 / XLS-70 specs, code review, integration debugging. Live group call (Zoom), recorded.
  • Thursday 16:00–16:30 CET — Product / GTM Office Hour (XRPL Commons + Aquarium alum). Topics: positioning, pitch shaping, distribution strategy, RLUSD/CEX integrations. Live group call, recorded.
  • On-demand AMA — async via dedicated Discord channel. ≤24h response from a rotating mentor pool of ~10 people.
  • 1:1 office hours bookable via shared Calendly (max 30min/week per team) — for product strategy, IP/legal, fundraising prep.
  • Recorded archive of all sessions, accessible to all teams + future reference.

Why 2 fixed slots: creates a heartbeat for the cohort, makes mentor scheduling sane, lets teams plan their week. The async Discord covers everything else.

Alternatives
  • Daily 30-min standup
    Every weekday morning, all teams join. Pros: high engagement, daily momentum. Cons: heavy on mentors (5h/week), risks performative attendance, hard for global timezones.
  • Async Discord-only
    No live calls, dedicated channel + mentors check in. Pros: lightweight, timezone-friendly. Cons: no "magic moment" of live problem solving, lower energy, cohort doesn't bond.
  • Weekly group AMA
    One 90-min session per week with all teams + 4 mentors panel. Pros: minimal overhead. Cons: doesn't scale (too many teams = no time per team), 1-week feedback loop too slow for a 4-week build.
  • Office Hours by theme
    One slot per Master Atlas theme per week (Wallets, Lending, RWA, EVM, etc.) with theme-expert mentors. Pros: deep theme expertise. Cons: 6 weekly slots = mentor scheduling nightmare; teams in different themes don't cross-pollinate.
  • On-demand only (no fixed slots)
    Teams book mentors via Calendly when needed. Pros: max flexibility. Cons: no cohort heartbeat, mentor utilization unpredictable, shy teams under-use it.

Open questions for the committee

Decisions that don't fit into the 9 main sections
  • Visual identity — does the Buildathon get its own logo (variant of XB with "Build" suffix)? Or stays under the XRPL Builder umbrella?
  • Application platform — Devpost, Gitcoin, Typeform, or custom XRPL form? Determines the pre-flight cost + integration with Discord.
  • Mentor pool size & recruitment — target ~15 mentors (10 active + 5 backup). Mix of XRPL Commons / Ripple / Aquarium alums / external partners. Who recruits, how compensated?
  • Sponsorship outreach — Ripple (likely yes), Mastercard (RLUSD partnership existing), Securitize / Anodos (RWA), Evernorth (XLS-66). Who owns the convo, what tier of sponsorship?
  • Communication strategy — who announces, when (15 July hard deadline), through which channels (XRPL Twitter, Ripple Insights, devrel newsletters, crypto press)? Is there a "wave 1" in July + "wave 2" in September?
  • Demo Day format — pitch length (5min? 8min?), fishbowl Q&A or judges-only Q&A, livestream with audience interaction?
  • IP & licensing — does the Buildathon require open-source MIT/Apache for prize eligibility, or is closed-source allowed (with the team retaining IP)?
