CONTEXT This page is the second reading (Analysis 2 — external challenger) of the opportunities to build on XRPL. It synthesizes 3 documents produced by an external analyst from the 4 PWGs (RWA, Stablecoins, Lending, Identity) held in Paris in April 2026 and the Atka XRPL DeFi Report (Jan 2026).

METHOD 80 gap topics were identified across the 4 PWG sessions. After rigorous filtering against the current state of the ecosystem, 19 gaps are deemed "genuine" (24%) — the remaining 76% being already addressed by existing XRPL primitives. These gaps have been reformulated into 24 actionable Focus Areas, grouped into 6 Themes.

FINDING #1 The ecosystem mainly suffers from an awareness gap, not a protocol gap. Working groups repeatedly flagged as missing capabilities that were already shipped. The consequence: a unified capabilities registry (Focus Area #22) and pre-deployed testnet infrastructure (Focus Area #21) are multipliers before even thinking about products.

Cross-cutting findings (4 PWG × Atka)

Cross-cutting synthesis from 80 topics, 19 genuine gaps, 4 working groups
  1. Cross-jurisdiction KYC harmonization is the dominant theme. Florence Tison framed it as a constraint ("impossible in practice") in Stablecoins; the Identity WG framed it as the biggest opportunity ("Universal KYC"). Same gap, two angles. Tech is live (XLS-70 + XLS-80); regulators are the bottleneck.
  2. P-256 / secp256r1 signing: the highest protocol opportunity. Small spec, disproportionate impact — unlocks Apple Secure Enclave, Android Keystore, FIDO2/WebAuthn and most enterprise HSMs. Ethereum reference implementation (EIP-7951) already exists. Single most actionable XLS proposal.
  3. Lending dominates the protocol list: 5 of 7 protocol gaps come from the Lending WG. Reason: XLS-66 v1 is intentionally minimal — uncollat only, no time-locks, no fee splitting. Most addressable in an XLS-66.1 follow-up.
  4. Acceptance networks: "merchant acceptance for stablecoins" (Stablecoins WG) and "acceptance for digital proofs" (Identity WG) mirror each other — two network-effects problems. Mastercard pilot (RLUSD on XRPL since Nov 2025) = partnership-driven, not protocol-driven template.
  5. ISIN/CFI/LEI native fields (RWA gap #8) = probably the easiest XLS to ship. Concrete need (TradFi integration), straightforward spec (XLS-89 extension), no novel crypto work. Quick win.
  6. The 4 regulatory gaps (cross-jurisdictional) cannot be solved by Ripple, XRPL Commons or any protocol team alone. Multi-stakeholder, multi-year. Worth tracking, not actionable as engineering priorities.
  7. The biggest global finding: 80 raw → 19 genuine (24%). The remaining 76% are already addressed by existing or in-development XRPL primitives. Confirmation: the WG-perceived "missing capabilities" feeling is an awareness gap, not a protocol gap.
